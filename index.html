FAQ

Questions we get a lot.

Straight answers, including exactly where our numbers come from, so you never have to take a claim on faith.

An ATS tracks candidates through a pipeline you still have to run by hand. Kestrel screens, assesses and interviews candidates itself, then hands your team a ranked shortlist of people who've already cleared the bar. You can run it alongside your existing ATS or in place of it.

Yes. Every candidate is told upfront that screening and the initial interview are conducted by Kestrel's AI, what it's evaluating, and when a human on your team will get involved.

Every score comes with the reasoning behind it, so your team can override any call. Kestrel is built to narrow the field, not to make the final decision on its own.

Kestrel ships with job profiles across engineering, sales, operations and support, and new profiles can be added for roles specific to your team.

Most teams are live on their first role within a day: pick a job profile, connect where applications come in from, and Kestrel starts screening.

Every candidate for a role is scored against the same rubric, and every score shows the reasoning behind it, so your team can see why someone was ranked where they were and override any call. A person on your team always makes the final hiring decision.

The market context numbers on our site come from independent, India specific data: the most current we could find, and none of it from a company that competes with the kind of AI screening, assessment or interview product Kestrel is:

80% of Indian employers report difficulty finding the skilled talent they need, up 25% since 2014.ManpowerGroup, 2025 Global Talent Shortage Survey, India Findings (survey of 3,150 employers across India) →
74% of recruiters in India say it's gotten harder to find qualified candidates over the past year; 77% say hiring now involves too many stages; 8 in 10 plan to expand their use of AI in hiring.LinkedIn platform data, India hiring report, as reported by Business Standard, Feb 2026 →

The ₹ figures in our "cost of getting it wrong" calculator are Kestrel's own estimate, not a third party survey finding. We're not aware of a published Indian study that prices this out. We build it by applying roughly 70% of the role's annual CTC (inside the 30% to 200% of salary range commonly used in HR turnover cost research, covering re recruitment, salary paid during underperformance, exit costs and ramp up time for the replacement) to typical Indian CTC bands for junior, mid level and senior roles. Read it as a scenario, not a claim about what your last bad hire specifically cost you.

We picked ManpowerGroup and LinkedIn because their data is India specific and current, and neither sells the kind of AI screening, assessment or interview product Kestrel is. We don't cite competing recruiting software vendors (ATS, sourcing tools or AI assessment platforms) as a source for any number on this site, even when their published research is well done, since they have a commercial interest in how those numbers get used.
