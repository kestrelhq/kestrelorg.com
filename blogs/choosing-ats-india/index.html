How to Choose an ATS as a Seed-to-Series A Startup in India

Most "best ATS" lists rank tools by features. A startup is better served by working the other way round: write down how you actually hire, test two or three tools on one real role, check how you would leave, and price the whole thing in rupees including GST. This guide gives you the steps, a weighted scorecard and a two-week trial plan. It does not name a winner, because the right answer depends on how you hire.

  • Write a one-page needs brief first. It becomes your scoring sheet.
  • Decide which of three categories you need: a standalone ATS, an ATS bundled in an HR suite, or a platform that also runs assessments and interviews.
  • Compare pricing models, not just prices. Per seat, per open job and quote-only plans behave very differently as you grow.
  • In India, test the job-board and data-protection details that generic guides skip.
  • Ask how you export your data before you sign.
  • Trial two or three tools on the same live role for two weeks.

Step 1: Write a needs brief before you see a demo

Vendor guides agree on this step. Pitch N Hire's buyer's guide, which is a vendor guide, suggests listing hiring volume, team size, role types, budget, required integrations, compliance needs and approval workflow, then using the brief as your scoring rubric. Workology's guide adds that everyone who will use the system should be involved: hiring managers, finance, and whoever handles legal or compliance.

For a seed to Series A team, a useful brief fits on one page:

  • How many roles you expect to open in the next 12 months, and which are engineering.
  • Who touches a candidate: founders, hiring managers, one recruiter, an interview panel.
  • Where candidates come from today: Naukri, LinkedIn, referrals, your careers page, agencies.
  • What happens between application and offer: screens, assessments, interviews, scorecards.
  • What must connect: calendar, email, your HR or payroll tool.
  • Your monthly budget ceiling, all-in.
  • Anything you cannot compromise on, such as data location, access controls or audit logs.

If you cannot fill a line, mark it unknown. Unknowns are questions for vendors, not things to guess.

Step 2: Pick the category, then the tool

A team hiring five engineers a year has different needs from one hiring five a month. At low volume, a free or cheap tier can be fine. At high volume, the cost of stitching tools together and chasing feedback in chat starts to outweigh licence fees.

Step 3: Understand the pricing model before the price

JazzHR's startup roundup (vendor content, published 24 August 2026) lists tools whose pricing falls into a handful of patterns. The same product can be cheap or expensive depending on which pattern matches your hiring.

Three extra points for India:

  • Add setup, migration and 18% GST before comparing quotes, as factoHR advises.
  • Some listed prices are third-party estimates or converted from dollars. Get the rupee figure in writing.
  • Free plans are real but limited. JazzHR lists Breezy HR's free plan as allowing one active position and says you will likely outgrow free plans as hiring picks up. Check limits against your brief.

Prices change often. This guide quotes no vendor's price, and you should not rely on any blog, this one included, for current numbers.

Step 4: Test the India-specific details

Job boards

factoHR's guide points out that direct posting, sponsored posting and importing applications are different things that vendors often lump together. A tool may do one and not the others, and posting to Naukri or LinkedIn may need separate credentials or a paid account. Publish a test vacancy to the portals you actually use before you decide.

DPDP

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, your company becomes a data fiduciary and each applicant a data principal. The Rules were notified in November 2025, with main compliance duties expected around May 2027. Ask each vendor, in writing, where candidate data is stored, how long it is kept, how deletion and access requests are handled, who can see what, and whether there are audit logs. Ask for documents rather than verbal assurances. Whether the Act's employment provisions cover applicants is still debated, so take legal advice on notices and consent.

Support and time zone

Check support hours in IST, and ask what support is included in the plan you would buy.

Payroll handoff

If you use an HR or payroll tool, ask how a hired candidate moves into it.

Step 5: Ask how you leave

Pitch N Hire's guide advises confirming data ownership and clean export before you sign, not when you want to leave. Three questions cover most of it:

  1. Can I export every candidate record, note, score and attachment, and in what format?
  2. Is there an open API or webhook, or only CSV?
  3. What happens to my data after I cancel, and how fast is it deleted?

Step 6: Score the finalists

Take the weights from your brief. This set suits a small engineering-led team, so change it if your priorities differ.

Score each criterion from 0 to 5, multiply by its weight, and total out of 500. Treat any score below 3 on export or DPDP as a blocker, however good the total.

Step 7: Run a two-week trial on one real role

In week 1, set up the same role in each finalist and import 20 to 30 real or anonymised past applicants. Post a test vacancy, move a few candidates through every stage, and have two hiring managers give feedback.

In week 2, try the awkward things. Reject and reopen a candidate, reschedule an interview, pull a report and export everything. Ask support a hard question and time the answer. Then score each tool with the sheet above.

Use the same role and scoring window for every tool, which Pitch N Hire's trial guidance also recommends.

Red flags

  • No written answer on data export or deletion.
  • Pricing that needs a call before you can learn the basic model.
  • A demo that shows slides and never your scenario.
  • A job-board integration that "needs confirmation" when you ask which portals work.
  • Features you need that sit on a plan above your budget.
  • References offered only from much larger companies.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an ATS at seed stage?

Not always. If you hire a few people a year, a spreadsheet and a shared inbox can work. An ATS earns its place when candidates, feedback and decisions start to get lost, or when several people interview.

Is a free ATS enough for a startup?

Sometimes, for a while. Free plans usually limit active jobs, features or users. Check the limits against your brief and plan for the day you outgrow them.

Should I choose an ATS from an HR suite?

It can make sense if one record from candidate to employee matters most. Check whether the hiring features are as strong as the HR ones.

Which ATS is best in India?

No single tool is best. It depends on your volume, job boards, budget and whether you need assessments. Use the scorecard.

How long does it take to set up an ATS?

Pitch N Hire says self-serve tools can be live in hours to a few days and enterprise suites take weeks to months. That is a vendor estimate with no cited source, so test setup time yourself during the trial.

What should I ask about DPDP?

Where data is stored, retention, deletion and access requests, access controls and audit logs. Get answers in writing and take legal advice.
